---
title: hackathon streak
date: 2026-10-13
summary: my hackathon experiences and the lessons learned
---

this semester, i went on a hackathon streak, participating in 3 hackathons in the span of 2 months. each hackathon was a unique experience, filled with so many challenges, learning opportunities, and moments of creativity. although the lack of sleep and constant traveling were so exhausting that i just recently recovered from pneumonia, the experiences have been worth it.

my 1st hackathon was HackMIT. i traveled up to Boston alone on the OurBus, texting my teammates, who i had never met irl, about ideas on the way there. the 4 of us came from different parts of the country: georgia, florida, north carolina, and new york. i learned so much more about frontend design from our teammate who specializes in that area, and about integrating the Dropbox API into our project and connecting the frontend and backend. it was also my first time visiting mit and boston, and i loved the area; it was very different from nyc. although i only slept for 2 hours on a funky air mattress in my friend's dorm, it was a great experience, and i learned so much from my teammates and the hackathon itself.

my 2nd hackathon was DivHacks at Columbia. it was my second time attending, and i got to work with friends i already knew. we worked on a project that combines hardware and software: a smart mirror that displays the weather and time, can be controlled by voice commands, and talks back using elevenlabs! it was so fascinating to see the mirror come to life and work the way we had envisioned. we won best use of elevenlabs, and it was so rewarding to see our hard work pay off.

my 3rd hackathon was HackKnight at Queens College. i love flushing so much, and i got the chance to get cantonese rice rolls nearby, which were delicious. anyways, it was my first 2-day hackathon, and we made a project for people with dementia and their caregivers: a game that helps people with dementia remember their loved ones and their memories, and also gives caregivers a way to track their loved one's progress. through this, i learned more about the current state of dementia and how many people are affected by it. in fact, there are over 50 million people worldwide living with dementia. it was shocking to hear, and i realized the importance of creating technology that addresses health issues like this. it was great working with more backend technologies like tigerdata and snowflake, and playing around with facial recognition.

considering my health, i don't think i'll be able to attend any more hackathons this semester, but i'm grateful for the experiences i've had and the lessons i've learned. i met so many new people, learned so much about how different technologies come together to make amazing, meaningful projects, and had so much fun. i can't wait to attend more hackathons in the future and keep learning and growing as a developer!!
